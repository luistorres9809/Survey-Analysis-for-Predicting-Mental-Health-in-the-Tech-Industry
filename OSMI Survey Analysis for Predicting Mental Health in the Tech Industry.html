OSMI Survey Analysis for Predicting Mental Health in the Tech Industry.¶

  • Name: Luis Esteban Torres Perdomo

1.Introduction¶

Mental health of employees has emerged as a crucial aspect in organizations where the mental stress, pressure, and a high-productivity culture can lead to emotional instability. The World Health Organization has described mental health as an optimal state that allows an individual to cope with the usual demands of life, grow in their occupation, and become actively engaged in the social environment (World Health Organization, 2022). However, some results show that factors such as chronic work stress, overloading of work, or lack of institutional support have adverse effects on such status (Harnois and Gabriel, 2002).

Despite the efforts to de-stigmatize mental health, one of the strongest barriers to seeking help continues to exist, perpetuated to some degree by the perceptions of judgment, discrimination, or job-related consequences. Machine learning holds a methodological solution to help predict help-seeking in this regard. Such technology holds the ability to generate predictive models to deal with large data sets and detect subtle relationships between individual, organizational, and behavioral factors (Shatte et al., 2019).

Open Sourcing Mental Illness (OSMI, 2023) is a non-profit organization whose mission is to encourage education and support for mental illness across tech communities. OSMI has been conducting anonymous surveys of technology workers on matters of mental health at their workplaces for several years.

This project proposes using a machine learning model based on real surveys carried out by OSMI to create an analytical framework that helps in understanding and predicting the behaviors of employees about their mental well-being, such that other companies can take action to help their employees. In addition to helping identify meaningful patterns, this approach generates meaningful knowledge for organizations that wish to establish evidence-informed prevention and early intervention strategies.

2.Objective¶

Train a supervised machine learning algorithm to estimate the likelihood of an employee needing mental health treatment based on professional and personal profile information collected through OSMI surveys.

2.1 Specific objectives:¶

  • Study real data of mental health within a technological environment.
  • Clean and transform the data for analysis.
  • Train multiple supervised models.
  • Analyze their performance using tools such as GridSearchCV and metrics such as Accuracy and ROC-AUC.
  • Make inferences and draw actionable conclusions.

male_labels = ["male", "m", "male-ish", "maile", "mal", "male (cis)", "make", "male ", "man", "msle", "mail", "malr", "cis man", "cis male"]
female_labels = ["cis female", "f", "female", "woman", "femake", "female ", "cis-female/femme", "female (cis)", "femail"]
trans_labels = ["trans-female", "something kinda male?", "queer/she/they", "non-binary", "nah", "all", "enby", "fluid", "genderqueer", "androgyne", "agender", "male leaning androgynous", "guy (-ish) ^_^", "trans woman", "neuter", "female (trans)", "queer", "ostensibly male, unsure what that really means"]
def simplify_gender(value):
    value = str(value).lower().strip()
    if value in male_labels:
        return 'Male'
    elif value in female_labels:
        return 'Female'
    elif value in trans_labels:
        return 'Trans'
    else:
        return 'other'
df['Gender'] = df['Gender'].apply(simplify_gender)

5 . Preprocessing¶

The data was prepared for correct use in the different models.

  • The dependent (treatment) and independents variables are separated.
In [187]:
y = df['treatment'].map({'Yes': 1, 'No': 0})
X = df.drop('treatment', axis=1)

A preprocessor with two parallel transformations is created:

  • Numerical columns are standardised with StandardScaler (mean = 0, standard deviation = 1).
  • Categorical columns are encoded with OneHotEncoder, transforming them into binary variables.
In [188]:
categorical_cols = X.select_dtypes(include='object').columns
numerical_cols = X.select_dtypes(include=['int64', 'float64']).columns
preprocessor = ColumnTransformer([
    ('num', StandardScaler(), numerical_cols),
    ('cat', OneHotEncoder(drop='first', handle_unknown='ignore'), categorical_cols)
])
  • The data is divided into training (80%) and test (20%).
In [189]:
X_train, X_test, y_train, y_test = train_test_split(X, y, stratify=y, test_size=0.2, random_state=42)

6. Model Training and Comparison¶

  • Five supervised models were selected for comparison and selection of the best model for the project.
In [190]:
models = {
    'Support Vector Machine': SVC(probability=True),

     'Random Forest': RandomForestClassifier(random_state=42),

  • For each model a set of hyperparameters is defined which will be searched using GridSearchCV.
In [191]:
params = {
    'Support Vector Machine': {'classifier__C': [1], 'classifier__kernel': ['rbf']},
    'Decision Tree': {'classifier__max_depth': [5, 10]},
    'Gradient Boosting': {'classifier__n_estimators': [100], 'classifier__learning_rate': [0.1]},
     'Random Forest': {'classifier__n_estimators': [100], 'classifier__max_depth': [10]},
    'Logistic Regression': {'classifier__C': [0.1, 1], 'classifier__solver': ['lbfgs']}
}
  • GridSearchCV with cross-validation (cv=5) is used to evaluate each combination of parameters.
  • The models are trained and compared.
  • The accurrancy and ROC-AUC were taken into account to select the best project.
In [192]:
accuracy_scores = {}
roc_auc_scores = {}
best_models = {}

for name, model in models.items():
    pipeline = Pipeline([
        ('preprocessor', preprocessor),
        ('classifier', model)
    ])
    grid = GridSearchCV(pipeline, params[name], cv=5, scoring='roc_auc')
    grid.fit(X_train, y_train)

    best_models[name] = grid.best_estimator_
    y_pred = grid.predict(X_test)
    y_proba = grid.predict_proba(X_test)[:, 1]

    accuracy_scores[name] = accuracy_score(y_test, y_pred)
    print(f"Accuracy - {name}: {accuracy_scores[name]:.4f}")
    roc_auc_scores[name] = roc_auc_score(y_test, y_proba)
    print(f"ROC-AUC - {name}: {roc_auc_scores[name]:.4f}")
    print(classification_report(y_test, y_pred))
    print(confusion_matrix(y_test, y_pred))
    print()

8. Conclusion¶

  • The logistic regression model was the most suitable for the project. Gradient Boosting and Support Vector Machine were also excellent, suggesting that more than one model can be used depending on the usage context.

  • Family history, interference with work because of mental health frequency, employer benefits, and perceived anonymity upon treatment were the most important predictor variables.

  • The prediction model can be utilized by HR and welfare personnel to identify employees who would require early contact with mental health.

Limitations:

  • The results are self-reporting and drawn from voluntary questionnaires, the potential source of a bias.
  • They might be not generalizable across all parts or company culture. Some behavioural or psychological controlling variables have remained unquantified.

Future research:

  • Improve the model using further longitudinal or behaviour data in order to quantify over-time trends.
  • Apply the model in live pilot environments for its social as well as operating quantification of effect.

Overall, the project verifies not only the technical feasibility, but also the strategic value of machine learning to augment mental health training and in-workplace interventions. The technology has the potential to deliver healthy and balanced workplaces for the technology sector.
